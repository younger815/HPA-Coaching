About

Bradley Younger

Building the confidence back into movement — one honest assessment at a time.

For more than a decade, Bradley Younger has worked across nearly every layer of the health and movement industry — as a personal trainer, a remedial massage therapist, and now as a registered chiropractor. Each discipline taught him something the others couldn’t: how the body moves, how it holds tension, and how it heals. Bringing all three together was never really a choice. It was always going to end back on the gym floor, helping people move the way their bodies were actually built to.

His approach is grounded in biomechanics and structural advantage — understanding not just that something hurts, but why, and what to do about it. It’s a perspective few trainers apply, and few clinicians take outside the treatment room. Bradley sees it as the missing link between rehabilitation and real results: the ability to spot a fundamental flaw in someone’s movement and correct it, before it quietly turns into an injury or a limit they’ve simply learned to live with.

That perspective isn’t only clinical. While completing his studies full-time alongside work and raising a young family, Bradley learned firsthand what it takes to protect a healthy routine inside an already full life. Rebuilding his own consistency reignited the reason he got into this industry in the first place — not to chase aesthetics, but to bring back the confidence, and the enjoyment, that movement is supposed to give people in the first place.

As a registered chiropractor, Bradley understands that pain and stiffness are often the real reason someone stops moving altogether, long before motivation ever becomes the issue. With clinical insight guiding every program, he helps clients see what’s actually holding them back — and builds a path forward that’s realistic, sustainable, and, most importantly, enjoyable again.

“I know what it’s like to feel time-poor and still want to show up for yourself. My job isn’t just to get you moving — it’s to help you understand why your body feels the way it does, and build the confidence to trust it again.”

— Bradley Younger
